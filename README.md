# Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

An interactive chemistry laboratory report exploring how concentration, temperature, surface area, and catalysts influence the rate of chemical reactions.

This project transforms a conventional high school chemistry experiment into an interactive scientific research poster, combining experimental data, scientific explanations, and a dynamic particle simulator.

## Overview

The rate of a chemical reaction depends on how frequently particles collide and how many of those collisions are effective. This project investigates four major factors that influence reaction rates through experimental observations and collision theory.

The central scientific principle:

> Reaction rate is governed by the frequency and effectiveness of particle collisions.

## Key Features

- **Interactive Research Poster** — A structured academic poster presenting the experiment, methodology, results, discussion, and conclusions.
- **Section Focus Mode** — Navigate through individual sections for a more focused reading experience.
- **Dynamic Particle Simulator** — Explore collision theory through an interactive particle simulation.
- **Experimental Data Visualization** — Compare reaction times across different experimental conditions.
- **Zoom Controls** — Adjust the poster viewing scale.
- **Print / PDF Support** — Generate a print-friendly version of the research poster.

## Experimental Findings

The experiment investigates four factors affecting reaction rates:

| Factor | Experimental Observation |
|---|---|
| Concentration | HCl at 1M: 64 seconds; HCl at 3M: 57 seconds |
| Temperature | 31°C: 75 seconds; 41°C: 50 seconds |
| Surface Area | Uncrushed eggshell: 240 seconds; crushed eggshell: 65 seconds |
| Catalyst | FeCl₃ showed a significant reaction change, while NaCl did not show comparable catalytic activity |

### Scientific Explanation

1. **Concentration** — Higher concentration increases collision frequency between reacting particles.
2. **Temperature** — Higher temperature increases particle kinetic energy and the fraction of particles capable of overcoming activation energy.
3. **Surface Area** — Greater exposed surface area provides more accessible sites for collisions.
4. **Catalyst** — A suitable catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy.
